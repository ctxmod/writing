# British Spellings, Chosen Once

British spelling is a set of consistent choices, not a list to sample
from. The core sets: `-ise` by default with the older `-ize` variant
noted (`organise`; `organize` appears in Oxford-leaning texts — either
is correct, one per document); `-our` where American has `-or`
(`colour`, `behaviour`, `labour`, `neighbour`); `-re` where American
has `-er` (`centre`, `metre`, `theatre`, `fibre`); `-ce` for nouns
against `-se` verbs (`practice`/`practise`, `licence`/`license`,
`defence`); and `-l` doubling before vowel suffixes (`travelled`,
`cancelled`, `modelling`, `counsellor`).

## Rationale

A document that mixes `organise` with `recognize` signals that nobody
held the pen. Choose the variant family at the start — `-ise` is the
safer default for general prose — and apply it mechanically; the
`-l` doubling and the noun/verb `-ce`/`-se` splits are where drafts
drift, because both languages share the words. Where a proper name or
an established technical term fixes the spelling (`World Trade
Organization`, `Program Files`), the fixed spelling wins over the
house set.
