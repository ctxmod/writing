# British Formats

Dates run day-month-year, spelled or numeric: 4 October 2026, or
4/10/2026 where the compact form is understood. The 24-hour clock is
familiar in official, transport, and technical registers
(`14:30`), while everyday speech says "half past two"; both are
correct, and `a.m.`/`p.m.` styling is the import to avoid in formal
prose. Currency writes the pound symbol before the amount with no
space (`£4.50`), and percentages are written as separate words in
formal prose (`20 per cent`) though the symbol is standard in
technical and tabular material (`20%`).

## Rationale

Formats are the surface a reader's eye trips over first, and the
divergences are exactly where an American-trained writer defaults
wrong without noticing: the month-first instinct writes October 4,
2026 as 10/4/2026, which a British reader takes for 10 April, almost
six months off. Keep the numerals-and-words split from the shared number
rules, and hold each format family — dates, times, money, percent —
to one style per document, because a table that mixes `£4.50` with
"four pounds fifty" reads as unfinished rather than varied.
