# British Punctuation

Single quotation marks are the British default, with doubles reserved
for quotes inside quotes ('she called it "unfinishable", and she was
right'). Punctuation sits outside the closing quotation mark unless
it belongs to the quotation itself: The judge called the argument
'entirely hopeless'. Full points (full stops) in abbreviations are
kept where they end a sentence's rhythm and dropped where house style
prefers the modern open form (`Dr`, `Mr`, `BBC`) — one way per
document.

## Rationale

The outside-the-quotes rule is logical placement: the punctuation
belongs to the containing sentence unless the quoted material itself
carries it, which keeps quotations honest — the writer's emphasis
stays visibly the writer's. The serial (Oxford) comma is optional in
British style; take a side and keep it, because "my parents, Ayn Rand
and God" is funny exactly once. Watch the quotation marks around
scare quotes and titles: single quotes for a word used as a word
(the term 'island'), and let logic, not habit, place the final
punctuation.
