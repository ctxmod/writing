# Sales outbound — the doctrine applied to selling

A sales message is correspondence in the sense of [message_doctrine.md](../../../../correspondence/packages/message_doctrine/reference/message_doctrine.md), and every rule there holds for it unchanged: the motivated next step (§1), the recipient's decision first (§2), information chosen before words (§3), information order as a decision (§4), the relationship that actually exists (§5), precision about what is known (§6), the peer stance and the recipient's control (§7), credibility through testability (§8), short without skimpy (§9) and the working order (§10). This file states what the sales situation adds. Each rule is a test with its exception, per the base README's "tests, not bans".

## 1. A commercial step is still a motivated step

The test of correspondence §1 applies with one sharpening: a positive reply is not commercial progress. Attention, a friendly answer and interest in a topic are each short of trust in a specific claim and readiness to take a specific risk, and a sales message is judged by whether the recipient now has the grounds for exactly the step it asks for. The practical aim is more well-founded, feasible next steps in engagements that suit both sides, with credibility kept and total effort reasonable.

A clear no is a qualification outcome. It tells the sender the situation is not a fit, or not now, and it spares both sides further work; a sales message that makes no hard to say fails correspondence §7 as well as this rule.

## 2. Text cannot repair a missing reason to sell

Writing cannot compensate for missing demand, a wrong recipient or a weak offer. When drafting shows that one of these is the problem, the right move is to surface it and stop, and a better-worded message over it fails the test. This is correspondence §1's "no reason to write" applied to the commercial case, and it is why the outbound working order in [drafting_order.md](drafting_order.md) lets a draft end in no message at all.

## 3. Diagnose, show, offer, let them decide

The posture that keeps a sales message from reading as a sales pitch is a professional's posture: someone good at the work who happens to be available for it. Four habits make it visible, and each is a test on the draft.

A diagnosis leads. The message opens with an observation or a question about the recipient's actual situation, and the sender's own offer appears only as the reason the sender can help with it. A draft whose first substantive content is the sender's offering fails.

The work is shown. Every quality the sender would claim is replaced by a concrete thing built, a result or a checkable artifact, per the base [show_qualities.md](../../../../core/packages/claims/rules/show_qualities.md); the outbound application is in [credibility_and_offers.md](credibility_and_offers.md).

Interest is stated once. The message offers one step, makes it easy, and then stops; chasing is the salesman's tell. The follow-up discipline that keeps this true over a thread is in [replies_and_follow_up.md](../rules/replies_and_follow_up.md).

The offer is narrow. A specific competence for a specific problem reads as expertise, while breadth ("solutions for all your needs") reads as hunger. The test: could the recipient tell, from the sentence describing what the sender does, which of their problems it would and would not cover?

Exception: when the recipient has asked what the sender offers, the offer is the answer and opens the reply, per correspondence §2's exception.

## 4. Readiness for it not to be a fit

A sender who genuinely wants the right engagements, and not every engagement, writes differently: the easy out is meant, the price is stated without pressure, and a no is accepted as information. The test is whether the draft would still read as written if the recipient declined. A message whose tone depends on a yes (insistence, urgency, an argument pre-empting the no) fails.

## 5. The hard part, named as a hypothesis

A sales message may name the hard part of the recipient's situation: the failure mode one layer below what they have said, which is where the sender's understanding shows. It is framed as a hypothesis about the domain or the situation, never as a presumed truth about the reader. The test: does the sentence claim to know the recipient's problem, or does it state what tends to go wrong in situations like theirs, leaving them to say whether it applies? The first fails both this rule and the stating-register test in [tone_and_register.md](../../../../core/packages/basics/reference/tone_and_register.md); the second passes. Correspondence §6's placement of uncertainty governs the wording: the uncertainty sits in the clause about the recipient, and the domain observation itself is stated plainly where it is well founded.

## 6. Commercial identity is never masked

A sales message is recognizably commercial. The sender's company need not be named in the opening, and a short sender introduction may follow later when it helps the recipient, but the commercial purpose is never disguised as a private matter, a customer issue or an existing relationship. The test is the forwarding test of [outbound_pre_send_checks.md](../rules/outbound_pre_send_checks.md): would a third party reading the message see at once that it comes from a seller? The legal identity required by the consuming repository's compliance controls appears where those controls require it.

## 7. Drafting with assistance

Assistance with research, structure, drafts and critique is allowed in internal work. The human sender owns the facts, the interpretations, the choice of recipient, the wording and the send. A statement about what a recipient feels, wants to hear or will react to, whoever produced it, is an uncertain hypothesis and never insight into the person; it may steer a choice of wording only as a hypothesis, and it never appears in the message as fact. Sensitive material is not moved into a tool merely to ease drafting. This is compatible with the base policy in ⟦PRIV-MD:../preferences/production-process.md|AB/.ctx/profiles/writing/reference/writing/preferences/production-process.md⟧, which keeps the public text silent about how it was produced.
