# Introductions and referrals

An introduction carries a third party's trust into a first contact, which makes it the strongest opening a sales message can have and the one most easily abused. This file covers asking a contact for an introduction or a referral, and writing the first contact that follows one. The first contact itself also follows [first_contact.md](first_contact.md).

## Asking for an introduction

A referral is earned and never assumed. The sender asks only on a real basis: after a good conversation, even one that will not become an engagement now, after delivering something genuinely useful, or when a prospect declines warmly ("the timing is wrong for us, though I'd like to stay in touch"), which is itself an opportunity to ask.

The ask is specific about the problem-owner sought. "Do you know anyone who is trying to get a matching pipeline production-ready, especially in logistics?" passes; "know anyone who needs help?" invites a vague non-answer and fails. The ask makes the yes effortless: the sender offers a short, correct, forwardable basis the contact can simply pass on, saying what a first exchange would concern, or asks for a name the sender may approach directly. The sender gives before asking, in the same message: a useful link, a thought, or thanks that is clearly genuine. There is one ask and no pressure, and when the contact does not take it up, the sender leaves it warmly.

The forwardable basis is written to survive the forwarding test in [outbound_pre_send_checks.md](../rules/outbound_pre_send_checks.md): a stranger reading it learns who the sender is, what problem they work on, and what a first exchange would cost the recipient.

## The introducer vouches for nothing they do not know

The introducer must never have to vouch for more than they actually know. A message sent after a referral may state the referral ("Erik Lund suggested I get in touch about …"), and it never claims or implies that the referral endorses the sender's delivery ability when the contact only passed on a name. The test: would the introducer, reading the message, agree with every sentence that mentions them?

## The referred first contact

A first contact made through a mutual acquaintance counts as warm. The warm register is allowed in it: the greeting and sign-off may be the pair the relationship actually uses, the greeting may carry an exclamation mark, and the tone may be warmer than a cold email's. The referral is the hook: it is true, specific and personal, and it opens the message. Everything else in [first_contact.md](first_contact.md) about information, credibility and the subject line still applies.

The referred first contact also takes the warm shape, even when its purpose is commercial: two or three plain questions and no call-to-action, as correspondence `messages.md` sets it out, with the reply as the next step. The four-part shape of [first_contact.md](first_contact.md), with its one offer and easy out, governs the cold first contact and not this one.
