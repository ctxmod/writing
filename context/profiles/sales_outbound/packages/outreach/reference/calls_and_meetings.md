# Calls, meetings and spoken drafts

A call or a digital meeting is a channel for what text handles badly: a need that is hard to interpret, a demonstration, or a disagreement. The channel choice is made at the nästa-steg step of [drafting_order.md](drafting_order.md); this file holds the discipline once a call or meeting is chosen, and the form of drafts written to be spoken.

## A call

An unknown caller identifies themselves at once and says what the concrete question concerns, because a call arrives without the visible sender an email has. A call is never framed as a follow-up when no relevant earlier contact has happened. A call is never used to get around a stated no.

## A meeting

A meeting is booked for a named purpose: understanding a workflow together, reviewing a test or a work sample, choosing between alternatives. The groundwork the meeting needs is sent beforehand, and the meeting is never a presentation read aloud to the recipient. Viewing a work sample is never made conditional on booking a meeting.

The meeting opens with the question or decision it exists to resolve, leaves room for the sender's assumptions to be corrected, and closes on what is decided, what remains, and who does what. A short written summary follows, and silence after a meeting is never taken as a needed approval. That summary is often the first written message of a thread that began by phone, and the utfall step of [drafting_order.md](drafting_order.md) starts from it.

An objection raised in a meeting or a call is answered with a cited fact. Where no verified fact answers it, the objection is recorded as an open question and answered later; it is never met with a claim the sender cannot support.

## Drafts written to be spoken

A call opener, a voicemail and a short email composed alongside them are drafted in a plain spoken register, read aloud before use: one ask per draft, no marketing words, no symmetric or balanced constructions ("both … and", paired clauses built for rhythm), and no bullet lists in an email. In Swedish this is the plain-spoken register of `talspraksnara_utskick` (module `writing_sv`, profile `korrespondens`, package `saljutskick`), which also governs written outbound drafts; written English outbound drafts take the same register, per [first_contact.md](first_contact.md). The test for a spoken draft is whether the sender would say it in those words on the phone; a sentence that only works on the page fails.

The briefs, scripts and word-count bounds a consuming repository uses to prepare calls and meetings are its mechanics, as are recording consent and the material a demo may show.
