# Credibility, offers and price

Correspondence §8 sets the general rule: credibility comes from what the recipient can check, and every channel the recipient may compare describes the same ability ([message_doctrine.md](../../../../correspondence/packages/message_doctrine/reference/message_doctrine.md)). This file applies it to the claims a sales message makes about the sender and to the offer and its price. The consuming repository keeps the registers this file presupposes: which outward claims are permitted, which offers exist at which status, and what they cost. The doctrine here governs how those claims and offers are written; it never supplies their content.

## Shown, never claimed

Every adjective the sender would apply to themselves ("experienced", "passionate", "proven", "world-class") is replaced by a concrete thing built, a result, or a checkable artifact, per the base [show_qualities.md](../../../../core/packages/claims/rules/show_qualities.md). One earned credibility line beats a paragraph of self-description. A result figure stated in the first person is allowed when it is true and substantiated (recorded in the base `overrides.md`). The proof is something the recipient could check: a work sample, a public artifact, a description precise enough to be asked about.

## The sender's own history

Credibility is literally true. Every statement about customers, experience and the delivery organization means exactly what it says, and a question about client history is answered directly.

The sender's own verifiable career history may carry credibility for a company the sender solely owns, stated as the sender's own history. The test: is the line about what the sender personally has done, and could the recipient verify it? Three limits keep the line honest. A former employer's customers are never presented as the company's customers. A former colleague's reference is a reference to past work, never a client testimonial for the company. When relevant history cannot be shown, the message says so plainly, and a confidential-client explanation is never invented.

A span of career is stated in the form that is literally true. A start year ("since <year>") states continuity; a duration ("a decade") overstates when part of the span was part-time or interrupted, and where a quantity is needed, the conservative figure that holds is used. Concrete career work carries more weight than a single academic project, which is cited only where it is directly relevant. Which career facts a given sender may state, and whether a company's claims register permits them, belongs to that sender's repository.

## The offer is stated plainly

The offer is named plainly and the message stops: no urgency, no limited availability, no asking twice. A message never promises a demo, report or analysis that does not exist when it is sent. A decision about scope, rate or constraint is stated with its one real reason and not defended further (correspondence `messages.md`).

## Price

Price is stated when it is needed to assess the offer, and a price question is answered directly; it is never held back behind a requirement to book a meeting first. The economics are named early, before the sender does extensive unpaid analysis, so neither side invests in an engagement whose price would end it.

The price covers what the recipient needs to reach the goal. When an offer is one phase of a larger path, the message states the sum of the parts the recipient will need, the ongoing costs and the recipient's own work, and never presents one phase as the whole investment. The test: after reading, could the recipient estimate what reaching the goal will cost them in total?

A price is stated as a number, without flinching and without over-explaining. When budget is the obstacle, scope is reduced, not the rate; a discount offered to win reads as a sign that the first number was not real. The names, amounts and statuses of offers come from the consuming repository's offer register.
