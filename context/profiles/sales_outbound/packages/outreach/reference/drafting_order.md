# The outbound working order

Correspondence §10 sets the working order every message follows, from intent to outcome ([message_doctrine.md](../../../../correspondence/packages/message_doctrine/reference/message_doctrine.md)). Sales outreach uses the same order, **intent → relationship → groundwork → information selection → next step → wording → review → outcome** (in Swedish sales teams often *avsikt → relation → underlag → informationsval → nästa steg → formulering → granskning → utfall*), and this file states what each step adds for a sales message. Wording still comes after the content and relationship decisions.

## The steps for a sales message

Intent (*avsikt*). The commercial reason to write must also be a reason for the recipient to read: a contribution relevant to them, a response they can be expected to give, or a request they need to handle. The sender's need, whether for a customer, a reference or evidence that an offer works, is not by itself a contact reason. Without a recipient-relevant reason the draft ends here, with no message, until the reason is better.

Relationship (*relation*). Before a first contact or an important sales message, the sender writes down what is actually known about the relationship: what has happened between the parties, what both know, which of the recipient's responsibilities or stated goals the message touches, which part of the sender's ability the recipient can already judge, what the recipient can actually decide, what a wrong yes or a reply costs them, why they would see the sender as a legitimate commercial party, and what they last asked, declined or ordered. That note is the relationship card (*relationskort*). Each entry is marked as known, a reasonable assumption, or unknown, with its source where it needs one, and no invented estimate of anyone's trust, interest or feelings is attached to it. The note is updated, never restarted, and the latest actual reply overrides any older summary. The record format, its keys and where it is stored belong to the consuming repository.

Groundwork (*underlag*). The groundwork names the concrete observation or thought that makes the contact worth judging, and keeps what is known apart from what is assumed and what is not known (correspondence §6). A sales message whose groundwork is only the sender's offer has no groundwork.

Information selection (*informationsval*). The four placements of correspondence §3 decide what the message carries. For a first contact, an offer or a price question, the selection is written down per candidate item before drafting: what the item is, its ground, its source and currency, the recipient question it answers, the wrong conclusion it prevents, and its placement. This is the information card (*informationskort*), a selection aid that the message never has to carry. Two outbound items are always considered: the most important limitation of what is offered, which is noted so that it survives shortening, and the price, which is placed under the price rule in [credibility_and_offers.md](credibility_and_offers.md) whenever the purpose of the message is commercial.

Next step (*nästa steg*). One main action, or none. Email is the standing default channel for facts, options and approvals; a call or a digital meeting is proposed when it concretely helps the parties understand a need that is hard to read, a demonstration or a disagreement, and it is never used to get around a stated no. Decisions made by phone or in a meeting are summarized in writing afterwards. The step must be possible for the recipient and must hide no larger commitment than the message states; a first yes is never a hidden order for more contact. A consuming repository may scope a different channel default to a named campaign; such a departure is its mechanics, recorded there with its scope.

Wording (*formulering*). The subject line and first sentence form one orientation, built under [first_contact.md](first_contact.md) when the message is a first contact. Then only the context and reasons the step needs, and the step at the end. No automatic company presentation, no merits paragraph and no meeting link pasted in by default.

Review (*granskning*). The sentence-by-sentence and whole-text review of correspondence §10 step 7, read as the recipient and as a sceptical third party. In a sales message the whole-text pass matters most, because individually reasonable sentences can add up to a text that sells. The checks are in [outbound_pre_send_checks.md](../rules/outbound_pre_send_checks.md).

Outcome (*utfall*). The human sender approves recipients, facts, wording and commitments before anything is sent; drafting tools draft and never send. What is recorded afterwards keeps what the sender believed apart from what the recipient actually did, and the next contact starts from that actual outcome, never from a pre-set selling sequence.

## Scaling the effort

A confirmation needs a quick check. A first contact needs the relationship card and the information selection. An offer, a security answer, a dispute or a delivery problem needs more. The order exists to prevent unconsidered sales messages, and it does not turn every email into a project.

## Scaffolds

A scaffold, a reusable message skeleton kept by the consuming repository, is never sent as it stands. Each send is drafted individually from it: every placeholder is replaced by a verified fact for this recipient, one message at a time, and none is filled from assumption. A scaffold never promises a demo, a report or an analysis that does not exist when the message is sent. Scaffolds are edited toward the working version of the worked contrast in [first_contact.md](first_contact.md), never toward the failing one. The scaffold texts themselves, their statuses and their map belong to the consuming repository.
