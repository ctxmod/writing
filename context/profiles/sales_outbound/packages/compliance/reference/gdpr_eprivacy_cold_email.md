# GDPR and ePrivacy for cold B2B email

The data-protection posture for B2B cold email in Sweden and the EU: the legitimate-interest basis, what keeps a cold email lawful, opt-out and suppression handling, and data retention. **This is operational guidance, not formal legal advice; when a genuinely uncertain case arises, treat it as a decision queued for the sender (or counsel), not something an agent settles.**

For a sender whose own offering involves care, compliance or regulated data, the outreach itself has to be exemplary: doing this right is part of the offer, and doing it sloppily would contradict it.

## The short version

Personal, relevant, one-to-one B2B cold email to a named professional about their own work is generally lawful in Sweden and the EU under the **legitimate-interest** basis of the GDPR, provided the message is genuinely relevant to that person's role, it is easy to opt out, and no more data is kept than needed. What is *not* lawful or acceptable: bulk unsolicited mail, irrelevant blasting, hiding who you are, or ignoring an opt-out. Outreach built small-batch, researched, personal, and with an out in every message stays on the right side of this by design.

## The legal basis: legitimate interest (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f))

For B2B prospecting the basis is **legitimate interest**, not consent. The standard three-part test, and how a well-run outreach satisfies it:

1. **Purpose** — offering a relevant professional service to someone whose job involves the exact problem the sender solves. Legitimate and commercially normal.
2. **Necessity** — a targeted email to the right named person is a proportionate way to reach them; nothing more is processed than name, role, work email, and public professional context.
3. **Balancing** — the recipient's interests must not override the sender's. This is why **relevance is the whole game**: an email about a problem the person actually owns is something they would reasonably expect and often welcome; an irrelevant one fails the balance. Targeting by *situation* ([situation_targeting.md](../../prospecting/reference/situation_targeting.md)) is what keeps outreach on the right side of this test.

Marketing email to a **named individual at a business** also engages the ePrivacy regime (in Sweden, marknadsföringslagen). B2B email to a person's role-based work address is permitted under legitimate interest **with a clear opt-out in every message**. Consent-first rules bite hardest for B2C and for personal, non-corporate addresses, which B2B outreach does not target.

## What keeps each email compliant (the checklist)

Every drafted email must satisfy all of these, and the first-contact craft ([first_contact.md](../../outreach/reference/first_contact.md)) already produces most of them:

- **Relevant to the recipient's actual role.** No relevance, no send.
- **Genuinely individual**, written for one person, not a merge of many. (The no-bulk rule.)
- **Honest about who is writing.** Real name, real company, real reply-to. No spoofing, no fake "re:" to imply a prior thread.
- **An easy opt-out.** A plain line letting them decline future contact, for example "If you'd rather I didn't follow up, just say and I'll leave it there." This doubles as the email's confident "out".
- **Identifiable sender and business.** The signature names the sending company and its website; that satisfies the "who is this" requirement without a legal footer.
- **Proportionate volume and cadence.** Small batches, one follow-up maximum ([replies_and_follow_up.md](../../outreach/rules/replies_and_follow_up.md)).

## Opt-out and suppression (the hard duties)

These are absolute and override everything else:

- **Honor any opt-out immediately and permanently.** "Not interested", "stop", "remove me", or anything similar → that person and/or company is suppressed: no follow-up, no future touch, ever.
- **Maintain a suppression list** in the outreach repository, recording anyone who has opted out (name, email, company, and date). **Before drafting any email, check it.** Where there is any doubt, mark the prospect's record as drafted but blocked.
- **A non-reply is neither consent nor a refusal**: it is parked after one follow-up, and may be revisited only for a genuinely new, relevant reason.

## Data minimization and retention

- **Collect only what the outreach needs:** name, role, work email, company, and public professional context relevant to the situation. No private, sensitive, or scraped-at-scale data ([email_discovery.md](../../prospecting/reference/email_discovery.md)).
- **Public sources only**, recorded with their source link in the prospect's record, so the provenance of every personal datum is auditable.
- **Retention:** keep a prospect's data only while there is a live or plausible professional reason. Park stale prospects; periodically prune records for people who clearly will not be contacted again. Suppression entries are kept (that is their purpose) but minimal.
- **Right to object and access:** if a prospect asks what is held or asks for deletion, comply promptly. When the prospect record and the mail thread are the full extent of what is held, this is easy to honor. Queue any such request as manual work for the sender to action and confirm.

## Cross-border note

Emailing prospects elsewhere in the EU is the same regime (the GDPR is EU-wide; local ePrivacy nuances exist, but the "relevant, identified, opt-out, B2B" posture travels well). For prospects outside the EU, local rules vary (CAN-SPAM in the US is more permissive but still requires honest headers and an opt-out); the standard above already meets or exceeds those. When unsure about a specific country, queue it as a manual check rather than guessing.

## Why this is a feature, not a tax

A prospect who gets a precise, useful, easy-to-decline email from someone who clearly did their homework experiences the sender's care directly: a person who is careful, relevant, and respectful of constraints. The compliance posture and the sales posture are the same posture; following it is not friction but the offer, demonstrated.
