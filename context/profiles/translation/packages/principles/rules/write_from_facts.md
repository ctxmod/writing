# Write Idiom from the Facts

Prose in the target language is written from the facts it reports. A
sentence built on the source language's structure and then patched
into the target language keeps the source shape and reads as a
translation, even when every word is correct. The test: would a fluent
writer of the target language, knowing only the facts, have produced
this phrasing? Where the answer is no, start again from the facts.

Natural idiom can carry the source culture's sensibility (clarity,
modesty, an absence of hype) without reading as translated. The general
ideal of plain, precise diction is the core profile's
[diction.md](../../../../core/packages/basics/reference/diction.md);
what follows is its application to English written from Swedish
material. Prefer the established English term for the thing to a
literal rendering:

> "real journal data" → "real patient records"
>
> "carried the rest of the engagement" → "remained in use for the rest of the project"
>
> "queries held at volume" → "to support queries over large volumes of operational data"
>
> "maintained after me" → "so others could maintain the system"

A phrase that comments on a resemblance, where the sentence should
state the fact, is cut entirely: "close to the shape of my current
work" and "a platform team in the plain sense" both go, and the
sentence says what the work was. An unsupported universal ("any
model's context window") is replaced by the actual problem addressed.

The Swedish half of the rule (Swedish written from the facts) is in
the writing_sv module's core rules.
