# Application answers — answering a posed question

An application answer is written into a question someone else posed: a form field, a screening question, or a direct question in a message. Answer the question that was asked, at the length it was asked at, and stop. A one-line field gets one line, a stated character limit gets an answer comfortably under it, and only a field that asks for an account earns a paragraph. Open with the answer, never with a restatement or a preface; end without a summary; save unrequested reasoning for the conversation. One concrete fact outperforms a qualified paragraph. In a short answer box where a letter would go, the asked length wins.

Concede a genuine gap once, plainly and completely, in the field that asks about it; everywhere else it stays out entirely, as a clause, a parenthesis or a softening aside alike. A limitation the reader's decision rests on is material and is never omitted. Brevity never licenses overstatement: a qualifier is cut only when the unqualified sentence is still true.

A motivation question (why the applicant is interested, applying or drawn to the organisation) is answered with motivation, under the doctrine of a letter's opening in `cover_letter` (profile `job_application`, package `letters`), § Motivation carries the letter. The answer gives true, anchored reasons for wanting this work here, never puts the restated CV at its centre or opening, and asserts nothing about the organisation or the role that the applicant cannot know or check. Naming an area is not a reason.
