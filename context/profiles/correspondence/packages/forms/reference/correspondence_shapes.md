# Correspondence Shapes

Skeletons for the four highest-frequency correspondence jobs, plus
the register tiers for openings and closings. Every shape follows
the core discipline — governing point first, one ask, the next step
stated at the end.

## The request

1. Subject naming topic and ask.
2. One-line context: why this lands on this reader.
3. The ask: owner, action, deadline.
4. What you provide: the attachment, the link, the numbers — the
   reader should not have to ask for materials.
5. Close with the next step and thanks where earned.

## The status update

1. Subject naming the project and the state ("Q3 migration: green,
   one risk").
2. Headline state in the first line: on track, at risk, blocked.
3. Progress since last update, in bullets, newest first.
4. Risks and needs — each with an owner.
5. Next update date, so silence has a meaning.

## The correction or apology

1. What happened, stated plainly and early — no weather report.
2. Impact, quantified where possible.
3. What has already been done (the containment), then what happens
   next with owners and dates.
4. The prevention, one paragraph — a correction that ships without a
   prevention paragraph invites repetition.
5. An apology sized to the impact, without self-flagellation; the
   reader needs the fix, not the writer's feelings about it.

## The outreach (cold or reintroduction)

1. Why this reader: one specific, verifiable line.
2. The value or the ask, stated in the reader's terms.
3. The small step: a fifteen-minute call, a one-page summary, a
   simple yes-or-no question.
4. A close that costs the reader nothing to answer.

## Greeting and sign-off register tiers

| Tier | Greeting | Sign-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formal (external, first contact, bad news) | Dear Ms. Okafor, | Sincerely, / Kind regards, |
| Professional (the everyday default) | Hi Ana, / Hello team, | Best, / Thanks, / Regards, |
| Casual (established, internal, low stakes) | Hey Ana, / Morning all, | Cheers, / Thanks! |

Match the tiers at both ends: a "Dear Sir/Madam" opening with a
"Cheers!" close whiplashes, and so does the reverse. When in doubt,
drop one tier toward the professional row — it is the register no
recipient reads as wrong.
