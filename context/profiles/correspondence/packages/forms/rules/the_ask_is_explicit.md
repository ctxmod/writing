# The Ask Is Explicit

A request names its owner, its action, and its deadline, and it
sits where the reader cannot miss it: "Ana, could you confirm the
notice period against the 2024 policy by Thursday noon?" Context,
background, and reasoning stay separate from the ask, and a message
carries one ask wherever that is achievable.

## Rationale

Implicit asks fail politely: the reader absorbs the context, agrees
with the analysis, archives the message, and the work never starts —
nobody was asked. The three parts earn their place. The owner
prevents diffusion ("someone should" means no one will); the action
must be verifiable, because "look into it" cannot be finished;
the deadline converts urgency into a fact the reader can act on,
and a deadline without a date is a mood. When the ask needs
qualification, put the conditions after the ask ("by Thursday, or
flag Wednesday if the audit runs over") rather than folding them
into the sentence until the request disappears.
