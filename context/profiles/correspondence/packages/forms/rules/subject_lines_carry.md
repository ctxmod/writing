# Subject Lines Carry

The subject line states the topic and, where one exists, the ask —
in the space a reader triages with. "Budget numbers for Friday's
review: your sign-off needed by Thursday" beats "Quick question" in
every measurable way: it survives search, it sorts the inbox, and it
lets the reader decide without opening the message.

## Rationale

A subject line is the only part of an email every recipient is
guaranteed to read, so it carries the triage load for the whole
thread. The wasted forms are predictable: "Quick question" (about
what?), "Update" (to what?), "Hi" (why?), and the empty subject —
which reads as either spam or emergency. Write the subject after the
body, when the ask has taken its final shape, and front-load the
noun: `Retention notice: period corrected to 30 days` — the reader
scanning forty subjects catches the topic in the first word. For
replies that genuinely change direction, change the subject line;
the thread's label is a promise the message keeps.
