# Claims and evidence in letters and CVs

Every sentence of an application is a claim about the applicant's work, so the general doctrine in [claims_and_evidence.md](../../../../core/packages/claims/reference/claims_and_evidence.md) governs it all: statement type and scope, calibrated confidence, negative results, shortening without false impressions, responsibility at its true level, the purpose bar and the stop rule. The rules below are what letters and CVs add on top of that doctrine; where a rule here and a base rule seem to collide, the base wording governs and the collision goes to whoever owns the writing rules. Adaptation of one letter toward several employers changes selection and emphasis, and it changes no fact.

## Evidence follows importance

A document's most important claims get its clearest, most concrete evidence. Space is allocated by importance to the reader, and the project that is easiest to describe at length does not earn space by being easy. A production claim stands as an assertion only until it is joined by what was built, for whom, the applicant's own responsibility in it, and the observed usage or result.

Emphasis also matches the profile the document presents. A letter that opens as a production developer keeps its body about production work; the reader weights a document by its proportions, and a claim contradicted by the page's own shape does not convince. Balance is restored by shortening the overweight example, with the truthful delimitation kept while cutting.

## Transfer is delimited

A transfer claim names the specific work moments that transfer to the role's requirements — the infrastructure, the constraints, the kinds of decisions — and ends on the delimited connection. Task-equivalence claimed at the category level fails even when it is hedged, because the hedge does the work precision owes.

Sweeping (avoid, illustrative):

> Evaluating and quality-assuring AI solutions is largely the same kind of work as the model evaluation …

Delimited (better): the named moments — the infrastructure for model evaluation, the isolated compute environments, the handling of sensitive data — with the connection to the role stated as what remains after the choice.

## Results at usage level

The statement-type ladder in [claims_and_evidence.md](../../../../core/packages/claims/reference/claims_and_evidence.md) §2 — built for, tested with, used on — applies with its genre consequence: introducing working methods that colleagues actually use is a valid result at usage level, and no invented organizational change or productivity effect is owed on top of it. State the responsibility actually held and the step now sought; a bigger outcome demanded from the same evidence is invention. Where enabling infrastructure was the deliverable, the enabling is the achievement to state, and an account that stops at "built and deployed" stands.

## Clear responsibility at the level the role asks about

Clear responsibility is distinct from sole responsibility. Aggregate framings in which the applicant owns, sets and decides everything paint a solo builder, and a leadership claim made that way answers the wrong question. For leadership-facing documents the weight goes to clarifying priorities, developing colleagues, anchoring trade-offs, and work methods that survive without personal involvement — mentoring, review routines, test strategy, documentation. Deciding and directing-and-developing are stated apart (illustrative):

> I make the technical decisions.

> I am responsible for the technical direction, mentor developers, and set the routines for code review and testing.

The mandate-verb rule in [claims_and_evidence.md](../../../../core/packages/claims/reference/claims_and_evidence.md) §9 — a held mandate keeps its verb across revisions — is the standing check on both sentences.

## One main job per example

Each example carries one distinct function: one proves production responsibility, another proves a different capability. Examples that each try to show everything turn the document into a project summary rather than an argument for suitability. An example that carries an independent argument gets its own paragraph, and material belonging to one example's job is kept out of another's. Transitions between examples do the argument's work: a transition introduces the question the next example worked on, and the stacked formal connectives and the equating transition are governed by [claims_and_evidence.md](../../../../core/packages/claims/reference/claims_and_evidence.md) §6.

## Detail and result selection

Technical names support a narrative arc — problem, delimited responsibility, decisive contribution, consequence — and enumeration competes with the arc for attention. A detail is chosen by which capability the target reader needs evidence of; a named detail that supports no capability in the arc is cut. The same selection governs results, and the test is evidentiary value for the role: a concrete control mechanism outranks a funding round, and indirect commercial evidence is cut before direct usage evidence. Limited-but-real usage must never read as large-scale while it is being shortened. This extends "only list what you can defend", in [show_qualities.md](../../../../core/packages/claims/rules/show_qualities.md), from competence lists to running prose.

## Working under rules

Three experiences read alike and are distinct: working in a project where rules apply, translating given requirements into technical solutions, and assessing which requirements apply. They are kept apart, and regimes are never claimed to coincide without support. The constraint's concrete meaning in the work, with one real technical measure where one exists, carries the claim (illustrative):

> Data protection was a practical precondition of the work with sensitive customer financial data.

## Collaboration shown through described work

For team- and platform-facing claims, one concrete documentation, handover, shared-component or joint trade-off example outweighs another acronym. The described work is the evidence; the collaboration claim standing alone is an attack surface, per [show_qualities.md](../../../../core/packages/claims/rules/show_qualities.md) on shown qualities.

## A production example answers three questions

A production example lets the reader understand which work task the solution supports, what the applicant was responsible for in it, and how it was actually used. Technical components gain their meaning from that frame, and an informative specification outranks a component name: the workflow a solution feeds says more than the name of the channel it runs in.

Component without the work task (avoid, illustrative):

> At a logistics company I was responsible for further developing and deploying a machine-learning routing solution.

Work task and flow specified (better):

> At a logistics company I was responsible for further developing and deploying a machine-learning solution that proposes delivery routes, which dispatchers then review and adjust.

## The stable fact base

Responsibility, time periods, performed actions, causes and results are invariant across every document about the same project; only selection and emphasis vary. Each project keeps one fact base — who did what, what was built, what was actually used and tested, the observed results, with the method detail and delimitations — and each letter is a selection from that base, never a reinterpretation of it. Relevance editing that smuggles in a new claim, above all a causality shift, is the canonical violation. The check runs before any adaptation edit.

## Figures in the applicant's voice

A first-person result figure is allowed when the figure is true and substantiated, and a figure without substantiation is cut or scoped down to what the evidence carries. An honest measure is used where one exists; where none does, scope and outcome are stated in words rather than padded with a vague number.

Naming years beside a technology carries no rule here; a consuming repository may keep a local rule against it.

## What survives a cut, and what ends a review

The purpose bar in [claims_and_evidence.md](../../../../core/packages/claims/reference/claims_and_evidence.md) §10 sets the floor: a true, defensible entry still leaves when the purpose does not need it. Three keepables meet the bar when they meet it: a working link to a summary of the work, a third distinct example, and a technical detail that clarifies the argument. A change is never forced to satisfy a preference for uniformity; the actual defect is identified first and only what repairs it changes, per the stop rule, §11.

The deletion test is the genre's cut instrument: strike the sentence; if the argument stands without it and nothing the reader needs falls, the sentence stays out. Applied to relevance transitions it exposes the real defect — strike every sentence of the "exactly what you are looking for" kind; where relevance collapses with them gone, the example or the claim's scope is wrong, and the transition is left out either way.

Review runs in passes: relevance first, then factual accuracy — did responsibility, scope or causality grow in the rewriting — then language, tested by whether the text is sayable aloud at a first interview. A letter need not change just because it could be phrased differently.
