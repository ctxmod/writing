# Offer

An offer asks the client to accept a defined piece of work at a stated price on stated terms, and everything in it serves that one decision. It is built like an invoice, because the two share their skeleton: sender, client, the priced lines, moms and the total. Where an invoice asks for payment under an agreement, the offer describes an offer the client can accept, so it adds validity, scope and acceptance. The offer therefore has two parts: a front page that is the offer, and appendices that hold what the reader needs to understand the price and the terms. Acceptance is a term, not a part of its own. This file sets what each part must give the reader, on top of [commercial_document_principles.md](../../principles/reference/commercial_document_principles.md) and [appendix_rule.md](../../principles/rules/appendix_rule.md); the offer's design decides the layout.

The default for offers is that the offer stays on one page: details move to appendices, or to the project description, or are cut by the writing principles, whatever the page layout makes room for. An offer that grows is first checked against the placement tiers in [reader_order.md](../../../../core/packages/whole_to_detail/rules/reader_order.md), detail by detail.

## The front page is the offer

The front page carries the offer the way an invoice carries a demand for payment, and a reader who reads only this page holds the whole offer: who offers what to whom, at what price, until when. It holds the parties, the offer's date and validity, a short opening, the price lines with their summary, and the list of appendices. It holds no contact block; the parties' details are those of the parties block.

The opening gives the frame in one or two sentences: the client's goal in the client's terms and what the offer covers towards it, with the delimitation that keeps the reader from assuming more, after the model's document opening quoted in [commercial_document_principles.md](../../principles/reference/commercial_document_principles.md). The full description of the work is the project description's, and the opening points to it rather than summarising it. What the opening does not carry is the vendor's account of itself; credentials and method belong where they support a claim the reader needs to accept, per [show_qualities.md](../../../../core/packages/claims/rules/show_qualities.md).

The price lines are the offer's statement of the price, and they pass the three-part price test in [commercial_document_principles.md](../../principles/reference/commercial_document_principles.md) on the page itself: each line names what it buys, the lines sum to the net amount, and the summary gives the net amount, moms and the total ("Totalsumma"), with the F-skatt line as on the invoice. A line label is short enough to read as a line; what the line covers in detail is in an appendix or in the project description, pointed to from there and not restated here.

The list of appendices closes the front page. It names each appendix in the document with its page, and lists the project description last, by reference, as the document that describes the work. The list is an index, not the only pointer: each appendix is still pointed to from the point where it matters, per [appendix_rule.md](../../principles/rules/appendix_rule.md).

## The appendices explain the price and the terms

Everything after the front page is an appendix, and each appendix is there because the reader needs it to understand the price or the terms: what each price line includes, how the price was reached and how it is paid, an optional service, ownership and handover, the terms. The appendices are built from named sub-sections and open with their own frame, per [appendix_rule.md](../../principles/rules/appendix_rule.md). Detail about how the work is done belongs to the project description, and the offer's appendices point to the project description's section or appendix instead of repeating it, per § References between documents in [commercial_document_principles.md](../../principles/reference/commercial_document_principles.md).

The scope is stated in the appendix that describes what the price lines include: for each line, what is delivered, concretely enough that the reader recognises it when it arrives, and what is excluded where the reader would otherwise assume it. Each entry stays short and points to the project description for the detail. An exemplifying list, such as examples of what a block of time or a service can be used for, sits beside the scope it exemplifies, introduced by a sentence that says what it exemplifies, per the list rule in [commercial_document_principles.md](../../principles/reference/commercial_document_principles.md). Operating detail of a service, such as how unused capacity is handled, response times or the steps of a handover, goes to a named sub-section; the example of the hour-rollover rule under catalogue entries 5 and 6 of [catalogue.md](../../../../core/packages/anti_patterns/reference/catalogue.md) is the standard case.

The terms that bind the offer — validity, payment, what happens if the scope changes — are stated in one bounded place, the terms appendix, so that the reader can find them as a block and the rest of the offer stays free of conditions. A term is stated as a term. A sentence that justifies a term to make it more palatable is omitted, as the hour-rollover example under catalogue entry 5 shows, and so is a sentence that spells out a consequence the term already makes plain, per entry 11 of the catalogue.

## Acceptance is a term

How the offer is accepted is one of the terms: who accepts, by what act, and by when the offer is valid. It is stated in the terms appendix in the same stating register as the rest of the offer, and the signatures follow it directly, at the end of that appendix. Acceptance has no section of its own, and no list of next steps follows the signatures; what happens once the offer is accepted is stated where it belongs, as a term or in the project description's plan.
