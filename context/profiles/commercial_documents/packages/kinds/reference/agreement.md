# Agreement

An agreement fixes what each party owes the other, and it is read when the parties disagree as well as when they agree. Its first duty is precision: every obligation can be identified, attributed to a party, and checked. The funnel of [commercial_document_principles.md](../../principles/reference/commercial_document_principles.md) still governs the reading order, so the agreement opens with the parties, the subject of the agreement and its purpose, and each section opens with what it regulates; the rules below decide how the regulating text is written.

## Defined terms

A term whose meaning the parties could dispute is defined once, before its first use, and then used in exactly that form throughout. A defined term is never replaced by a synonym for variety, because a reader of an agreement is entitled to assume that a different word means a different thing. Definitions sit together in one place near the beginning, or beside the term's first use where the agreement is short; either way the definition comes before the obligation that depends on it, per the model's rule that prerequisites come before what depends on them.

## Obligations stated once

Each obligation is stated once, in the section that regulates it, with the party that owes it, what is owed, and when or under what condition. A second statement of the same obligation elsewhere, even a paraphrase, creates the question of which one governs; a section that needs the obligation refers to the section that states it. An obligation is stated with the party as subject and a verb that binds; a sentence whose subject is the work or the system leaves the debtor implicit.

## Order of precedence

Where the agreement incorporates other documents, such as an offer, a project description or general terms, it names them and states which governs when they conflict. The incorporated documents keep their own rules: the project description stays price-free, and the price is stated in one document only.

## No sales register

An agreement carries no persuasion, no justification of its terms and no description of benefits. Every sentence either defines, obliges, permits, conditions or states a consequence. A sentence that explains why a term is reasonable is omitted; if the reason matters to interpretation, it is stated as the purpose of the clause. The sales-register test that [commercial_document_principles.md](../../principles/reference/commercial_document_principles.md) points to applies with no exception for tone: a sentence that tries to move the reader has no place here.

## Appendices

Specifications, price schedules and service levels may sit in appendices, and the main text names each one and its place in the order of precedence. The appendix rule in [appendix_rule.md](../../principles/rules/appendix_rule.md) applies with one tightening: an appendix that carries obligations is part of the agreement, and the main text says so where it incorporates it.
