# Stand Behind Every Sentence

Every sentence of an outward text is one the author would say, in these words, to this reader in person, and could answer a follow-up question about. Experience, anecdotes, examples and feelings in the text are the author's own and true at the size stated; uncertainty sits in the sentence it concerns; the voice is one the author could keep up in conversation. Help from tools or people is welcome and leaves ownership with the author. A statement about how the text was produced is never false, and any policy that requires disclosure is followed.

The test before sending: read each sentence as its author, asking "would I say this to them, and could I defend it?" A sentence that fails is rewritten until it passes or is cut, whoever drafted it.

## Rationale

Readers trust a text as far as they trust the person they believe stands behind it, and the first follow-up question exposes a sentence nobody owns. Borrowed experience and performed feeling fail at once when noticed, and a reader who suspects that a text was generated on the sender's behalf discounts both text and sender.
