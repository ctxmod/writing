# Review test

Ask five control questions of a draft at every level (the whole document, each section, each paragraph), top-down, and fix the frame before descending:

1. Can the reader understand early what this part is meant to convey?
2. Does the reader have the concepts and prerequisites that what follows requires?
3. Is it clear why the details are there and how they fit together?
4. Is there too large a jump between the overall idea and the details?
5. Does the opening give a usable frame, or is it only a declaration of content?

Then read once straight through as the intended reader, watching for the late-relevance symptom: an earlier fact that only now makes sense. Repair it locally by moving the explanation forward, stating the relation where the fact first appears, or adding one orienting sentence, rather than adding general background. A draft passes when every level answers all five questions and a straight read surfaces no late relevance.
