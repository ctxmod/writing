# Reader order

A text has an order of emphasis whether or not the writer chooses one. Choose it: the reader meets the frame first, then the parts it governs, then the detail, in a funnel from wide to narrow. This rule states that choice and the placement test that follows from it. Both rest on the whole-to-detail model in [whole_to_detail.md](../reference/whole_to_detail.md) and on the principle that details the reader does not need move to an appendix or out of the text.

## The funnel

The funnel is the model's ordering rule applied to emphasis. What the reader must understand to read the rest comes first: the purpose, the main point, the relation that unites the parts. Each following step narrows, and each narrowing is justified by the step above it. The reader should be able to stop at any level and still hold a correct, if coarser, picture.

Two consequences follow. First, the position of a sentence signals its weight; a central claim buried after its own details reads as an afterthought, and a minor detail placed first reads as the point. Second, the funnel governs every level the model names, so a section and a paragraph each open with their own frame.

The funnel orders understanding, and the model's §5 marks its boundary. A conclusion may come first when the intended reader can understand it without preparation. When it depends on categories or assumptions the reader does not yet hold, those come first, or are explained right beside the conclusion.

## Information placement

Each piece of information belongs in one of four places, and each place has its test.

### Say now

The reader needs this here to understand what follows, or to decide or act at this point. The test is dependency: if removing the sentence leaves the next passage unclear or the reader unable to act, it stays where it is. This is the model's "prerequisites before dependents", read as a placement rule.

### Make available

The reader will need this, but later. It belongs in the main text at the point where it first carries weight, and it moves there. The test is timing: if the information only becomes meaningful after something the text has not yet established, placing it now produces the late-relevance symptom described in [review_test.md](review_test.md).

### Appendix

Some readers will want this, but the main line does not depend on it. It goes into a specific sub-section of an appendix that can be read when needed or out of particular interest. The test is optionality: the main text reads completely without it, and a reader with a specific interest can still find it.

### Omit

No reader of this text needs it. Internal structure, process narration and inventory counts often land here: they describe how the text or the work was organised, and they give the reader nothing to understand or act on. The test is consequence: if no reader would understand, decide or do anything differently with the information, it goes.
