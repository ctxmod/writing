# Reader order

Choose the order of emphasis: the reader meets the frame first, then the parts it governs, then the detail, in a funnel from wide to narrow at every level from document to paragraph. A reader who stops at any level still holds a correct, if coarser, picture. A conclusion may come first when the intended reader understands it without preparation; otherwise its prerequisites come first or right beside it.

Place each piece of information by its test. Say now what the next passage or the reader's next action depends on. Make available later what only carries weight after something the text has not yet established. Move to an appendix what some readers want but the main line does not need. Omit what would change no reader's understanding, decision or action.
