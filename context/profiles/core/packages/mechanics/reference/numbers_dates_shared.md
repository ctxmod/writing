# Numbers, Dates, and Shared Ground Rules

Numbers and dates carry two layers of convention: the ground rules
both major English dialects share, and the format divergences — date
order, clock style, currency placement — the dialect profiles'
conventions packages carry. This reference fixes the shared layer.

## Numbers

Spell out one through nine in general prose and use numerals from 10
up (`three options`, `11 files`); keep numerals for anything with a
unit or a precise value (`4 GB`, `6 percent`, `page 7`), for all
numbers in technical or financial material, and for any two numbers
compared side by side ("between 8 and 12 threads" — do not write
"between eight and 12"). Never open a sentence with a numeral:
rewrite ("Twelve builds failed") or re-cast ("Some 12 builds
failed"). Rounded approximations spell out ("about two hundred
users").

## Dates

Write dates so no component is ambiguous when read cold: spell the
month wherever the day-month or month-day order is not carried by the
surrounding convention. The compact numeric forms differ by dialect
and stay with their dialect profile; the shared rules are: never
invent an order the reader cannot recover, use four-digit years
(`2026-10-10` is unambiguous; `10/11/12` is not), and keep one date
format within a document.

## Times

Numerals with a colon for clock times (`09:30`, `14:00`); the
meridiem styling (`a.m.`/`p.m.` versus the 24-hour clock) is a
dialect divergence. State the zone whenever the audience spans one
(`14:00 UTC`), because an unzoned time is a guess.

## Currency

Amounts take numerals and the currency symbol attached to the first
figure (`$40`, `£12.50`); symbol placement and which symbol is home
default are dialect divergences. Spell the currency in words when the
text discusses the currency itself ("the dollar fell").

## Ranges and precision

Ranges join with "to" or a dash, consistently (`pages 40-55`);
significant figures are a promise — `3.14` claims more precision than
"about 3" and both claim less than `3.14159`. Round to the precision
you can defend, then hold that precision across comparisons.
