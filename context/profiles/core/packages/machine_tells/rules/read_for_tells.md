# Read for Tells

Before an outward text leaves, read it for the patterns readers associate with machine-generated text. They are stock vocabulary, inflated significance, commentary tails, manufactured contrast, habitual triads, hedge fog, vague attribution, service openers and closers, summary closes, default formatting, uniform rhythm, performed feeling, drafting residue, and above all text that would read the same to any reader. Each hit is repaired by writing the specific fact, decision or reason the pattern stood in for, or by cutting the sentence. A text is never disguised to look human, and a feature that is not a reliable tell (correct grammar, a formal register, a dash, a real list of three) is left alone.

When reviewing someone else's text, name the defect a tell points to ("generic", "unsourced", "restates the body") and never a suspected origin, because no tell proves who or what wrote a text.

## Rationale

Readers who suspect a text was generated on the sender's behalf judge it as less sincere and its sender as less trustworthy, whether or not a machine was involved, and a polished text without substance shifts the work onto its reader. The tells matter as symptoms, since each usually marks a place where the author had nothing specific to say.
