# Substance — shown qualities and defensible claims

Show a quality through context, action or result, and let the reader infer it. A quality adjective ("driven", "structured", "rigorous") stands only with its evidence in the same sentence or the next; interest and appreciation are carried by a concrete detail of the thing. Every claim, about the author or about the world, rests on context, action or result, or is cut, softened, delimited or marked as the author's interpretation. Direction is framed as direction, never as something achieved, and nothing is inflated or invented. A list entry passes only if the author could answer three follow-up questions about it; a thin entry is cut, not hedged. Use a number whenever a real, defensible one exists, and otherwise describe scope and outcome in words.
