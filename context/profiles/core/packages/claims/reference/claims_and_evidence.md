# Claims and evidence — the general doctrine

Applies to any claim an author makes about themselves, their work, or the world. This file governs how a claim is scoped, calibrated and shortened; `argumentation` (module `writing`, profile `prose`, package `argument`) governs claims made about and against someone else's position, and `working_with_sources` (module `writing`, profile `prose`, package `argument`) governs what the sources behind a claim actually establish. The genre-specific half of the doctrine (evidence follows importance, one job per example, working-under-rules precision, the stable fact base) lives in the genre profiles, chiefly `job_application`.

## 1. Delimited beats broad

A delimited, well-evidenced claim is stronger than a broader one the reader must take on trust. Name what specifically holds or transfers; even a hedged equivalence ("largely the same kind of work") is too sweeping. Precision comes from the right delimitation; maximal detail does not supply it. The nuance that makes a sentence correct can stay in the grounding so the sentence can stay short. Disclose method in proportion to the argument; omission is honest, implying the unperformed happened is not.

## 2. Statement type and scope

Before writing a result sentence, decide what kind it is: an observation (what was seen), an assessment (what was concluded, with its object and limits), or a possibility (what could be done, never stated as done). Keep intent, use, and outcome apart: what a thing is built to do is separate from how it is actually used, and actual use is separate from a measured effect. Built for, tested with, and used on are three different claims; the verb carries the level, and each level needs its own evidence.

Where enabling infrastructure was the deliverable, the enabling is the achievement to state; stating it is stating the work, and dressing it as an outcome beyond that inflates it. Separating these levels demands no numbers, and an effect is never invented to complete the account: an account that stops at "built and deployed" stands, and a fabricated effect does not.

The evidence level is matched to the claim: when the evidence improves, the claim keeps its scope and the gain goes into confidence. Better evidence never licenses a bigger claim, and a claim written to its evidence is not under-claimed.

## 3. Calibrated confidence

Strip uncertainty markers that only weaken (stacked "would probably / seems", vague framing coinages) and keep every delimitation that makes the claim correct. The test is whether removing the marker changes the truth. Avoid side-story comparisons that open questions the text does not answer. Correcting over-confidence must not install a false modesty that erases what the facts do carry.

## 4. Describe the investigated case before generalizing

A claim about a whole category is replaced by what was actually investigated: which cases, which data, which method. Generalization is the reader's move, and the text does not make it for them.

Category verdict (avoid):

> These models do not work on Swedish data.

Investigated case (better):

> The models we examined were trained on American records and needed
> adaptation to the Swedish data we had.

## 5. Negative results kept and scoped

A negative result is kept, neither hidden nor converted into a success story. State the decisive observation, then the delimited assessment with its object: a "no" to what, within what limits. Neither excuse nor victory lap. Runs and attempts that never happened are stated as never having happened; silence about them leaves the reader assuming they did.

## 6. The per-sentence control question

Does this sentence add evidence, explain relevance, or clarify motivation? If none, cut it. Connective tissue that leads the reader forward is content. A relevance sentence must add a connection, and asserting that one exists does not; an after-the-fact summary of what an example already showed is cut. Audit each paragraph's function before and after a revision: a cut that improves tone while silently removing needed information is not an improvement. The close opens the next step rather than summarizing again.

A transition introduces the question the next passage answers. Stacked formal connectives ("furthermore, in addition, moreover") signal connection without making one. A transition helps the reader move between two things, and it never equates them: "the same kind of problem" between two examples claims an equivalence the text then owes evidence for.

## 7. Simplify by choosing away

Shorten by selecting content away and specifying what remains, never by abstracting concrete words. The shortest phrasing is not always the most natural: an overloaded sentence is restructured (subject and action first, separate jobs separated), and a synonym patch does not do that work. After a deletion, audit the remaining text for dangling references to what was removed. Shortening removes whole facts; it never leaves a fact incomplete.

Shortening must never leave a false impression behind. Brevity that implies something untrue is worse than the paragraph it replaced, because the false implication is harder to catch than a false statement: every remaining sentence can be true and the shortened text still fail. Defensibility is checked on the impression the text leaves, sentence by sentence and as a whole.

A finished text carries no residue of its own drafting: superseded phrasings, transitions that once joined passages now removed, and alternative wordings left standing beside the chosen one are cut. The reader sees the text, never its history.

Competence categories fused together ("data and AI and cloud") read as template, and the fault is the unexplained technology, so the cure is saying what the work with it consisted of; technology terms themselves stay where they are exact.

A length target is a guide, and never itself a reason to cut. When a text runs long, cut repetition first (the thesis retold across sections, cases retold in the conclusion) and keep the qualifications that make claims correct (§ 3); reallocate the freed words to the limits of the claim before adding new material. A difference of a few percent from the target is not an editorial problem.

## 8. Mechanism over quality description

A mechanism the reader can picture outweighs a named quality. Once the mechanism is on the page, the conclusion the reader should draw from it is left to the reader. State the problem before the solution, so the solution reads as the answer rather than as a specification, and keep distinct motives apart rather than pressing them under one label.

A decision reason is given only where the reason shows judgment, and building from scratch carries no merit by itself: the merit is what the building achieved or what the decision weighed.

A property belongs inside the example it describes: an evaluative property stated beside an example it does not modify floats free and reads as self-assessment.

Concrete language clarifies relations — who acts, on what, and which part is whose responsibility — while adding more component names clarifies nothing.

## 9. Responsibility stated at its true level

Verbs of responsibility carry the actual relation: who bore it, alone or with others, and at what level. The actorless passive hides whose work the sentence describes, so the actor is named. A text that opens broad and corrects later has already made the broad claim, and the correction never fully displaces it; the precise verb comes first.

A mandate actually held keeps its verb across revisions. Successive edits soften "is responsible for" into "works with" (in Swedish, "ansvarar för" into "arbetar med"), and the softened verb understates a held mandate; the decay is invisible edit by edit, so the check compares the current verb against the mandate actually held.

## 10. The purpose bar

"Defensible" sets the floor for what may be said, and the bar for what stays in is the text's purpose. An entry that is true and defensible still leaves when the purpose does not need it. Cuts are made against purpose; truth decides only what may never appear.

## 11. The stop rule

A text need not change just because it could be phrased differently. A formulation that is factual, relevant, and sayable stands; the burden of justification sits with the change. Not all feedback warrants an edit: identify the actual defect, then change only what repairs it.

## How to apply

Run 1–3 whenever a claim is drafted, 4 whenever a generalization is drafted, 5 on every negative result and every run the text is silent about, 6 as the per-sentence cut test, 7 whenever text is shortened or carries drafting residue, 8 wherever a quality or mechanism is described, 9 on every responsibility verb and passive construction, 10 before anything is kept for its truth alone, and 11 as the bar that ends a review.
