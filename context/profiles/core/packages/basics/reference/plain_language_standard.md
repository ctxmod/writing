# The Plain Language Standard

Plain language has an international standard. ISO 24495-1:2023,
*Plain language — Part 1: Governing principles and guidelines*,
defines it by outcome, not by word lists: communication is in plain
language when its wording, structure, and design are so clear that
the intended readers can easily find what they need, understand what
they find, and use that information. The standard applies to printed
and digital documents that are primarily text.

## The four principles

The standard organizes its guidelines under four principles, each a
question about the reader:

| Principle | The reader… | This module's matching doctrine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relevant | gets what they need | identify the readers and their purpose before drafting; answer first |
| Findable | can easily find what they need | headings that predict, one job per paragraph, whole-to-detail order |
| Understandable | can easily understand what they find | plain words, concrete subjects, visible actors, defined terms |
| Usable | can easily use the information | evaluate the document as it develops, and with real readers |

## What the standard changes in practice

- **Success is measured on readers, not on formulas.** The standard
  focuses on how successfully readers use the document rather than on
  mechanical measures such as readability formulas. A readability
  score can flag a long sentence; it cannot show that the reader found
  the deadline. Test with readers, or with a reviewer who reads as one,
  when the stakes justify it.
- **Plain word choice is one guideline among many.** The `Prefer Plain
  Words` rule covers the understandable principle's word level;
  structure and design (headings, order, layout, tables) carry the
  findable principle and are as much a part of plain language as
  vocabulary.
- **Specialist readers still get plain language.** Plain language is
  defined against the intended readers, so a precise technical term
  that the readers share is plain for them; the same term in a
  document for the public needs a definition or a replacement.

## The other parts

ISO 24495-2:2025, *Plain language — Part 2: Legal communication*
(published August 2025), explains how to apply the Part 1 principles
in legal contexts, and covers legal documents in all sectors,
including the legal, governmental, non-governmental, and health
sectors. Writers of agreements and other commercial documents can
cite it when a counterparty or client asks why a clause was rewritten
in plain terms. National standards bodies adopt the parts as national
standards; Sweden, for example, publishes SS-ISO 24495-2:2025.
