# Write for One Reader

Before drafting, name the reader and state four facts about them. They are who the reader is to the writer, what they already know, what they will do, decide or feel after reading, and how much attention they will give the text. Then write to that person. Every sentence either answers something this reader needs or goes; every term they may not hold is defined or replaced; nothing they already hold is explained again. Where readers differ, serve the primary reader in the main text and place what others need where they can find it.

The test before sending: read the draft once as that reader. Mark where they would stall, where they would ask "so what?", and where they would notice the text could have been sent to anyone else. Each mark is repaired with something true of this reader, and a sentence about the reader states only what they said, what the writer observed, or what can be checked.

## Rationale

Writers model the reader badly by default. Knowing a subject makes it hard to imagine not knowing it (the curse of knowledge), most readers skim and give a text less time than the writer imagines, and a generic text tells its reader they were not worth the effort of a specific one. The model, the evidence and the tests for specificity and for being seen without flattery are in [reader_empathy.md](../reference/reader_empathy.md).
