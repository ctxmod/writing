# Headings That Predict

A heading describes exactly the content below it — no more, no less —
and parallel sections carry parallel headings in parallel grammatical
form. "Configuration Reference" promises a list of settings; if the
section opens with two paragraphs of migration history, the heading
lied or the content moved.

## Rationale

Readers navigate by headings, deciding what to read and what to skip
from the heading alone. A vague heading ("Overview", "Other notes",
"Details") forces them to read the section to learn what it is, which
is the navigation the heading was supposed to replace. Parallel form
makes scanning cheap: a document whose sections start "Install the
tool", "Configure the tool", "Test the tool" lets the reader confirm
coverage at a glance, while "Installation", "How to configure",
"Testing your setup" forces three decoding styles for one list. Fix
order: first make each heading truthfully describe its section, then
regularize the forms.
