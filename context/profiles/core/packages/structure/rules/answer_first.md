# Answer First

Lead with the governing point: the conclusion, the answer, or the
request stands in the first sentence or two, and the support,
qualification, and background follow it. The reader who stops after
one paragraph should leave with the document's main claim intact.

## Rationale

Readers allocate attention in a decaying curve, and documents are
skimmed, not recited. The newspaper lede and the executive summary
exist for the same reason: once the point is known, every supporting
sentence is easier to place — the reader knows what the evidence is
for. Background-first organization makes readers hold unfinished
context in memory while guessing where it lands, and most abandon
before the payoff. The exception is genuine narrative (a story that
depends on suspense), and even there the exception must be chosen,
not defaulted into.

Practical test: take the last sentence of your first draft's opening
paragraph — that sentence, moved to the front, is usually the
document you meant to write. `Q&A` shape, `BLUF` (bottom line up
front), and `TL;DR` blocks are all the same rule in different clothes.
