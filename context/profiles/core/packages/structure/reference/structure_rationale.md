# Document structure rules in full

The rules [answer_first.md](../rules/answer_first.md), [headings_that_predict.md](../rules/headings_that_predict.md), [one_job_per_paragraph.md](../rules/one_job_per_paragraph.md) state their obligations and tests in brief. This file holds each rule in full, with its rationale.

## Answer First

Lead with the governing point: the conclusion, the answer, or the
request stands in the first sentence or two, and the support,
qualification, and background follow it. The reader who stops after
one paragraph should leave with the document's main claim intact.

### Rationale

Readers allocate attention in a decaying curve, and documents are
skimmed, not recited. The newspaper lede and the executive summary
exist for the same reason: once the point is known, every supporting
sentence is easier to place — the reader knows what the evidence is
for. Background-first organization makes readers hold unfinished
context in memory while guessing where it lands, and most abandon
before the payoff. The exception is genuine narrative (a story that
depends on suspense), and even there the exception must be chosen,
not defaulted into.

Practical test: take the last sentence of your first draft's opening
paragraph — that sentence, moved to the front, is usually the
document you meant to write. `Q&A` shape, `BLUF` (bottom line up
front), and `TL;DR` blocks are all the same rule in different clothes.

## Headings That Predict

A heading describes exactly the content below it — no more, no less —
and parallel sections carry parallel headings in parallel grammatical
form. "Configuration Reference" promises a list of settings; if the
section opens with two paragraphs of migration history, the heading
lied or the content moved.

### Rationale

Readers navigate by headings, deciding what to read and what to skip
from the heading alone. A vague heading ("Overview", "Other notes",
"Details") forces them to read the section to learn what it is, which
is the navigation the heading was supposed to replace. Parallel form
makes scanning cheap: a document whose sections start "Install the
tool", "Configure the tool", "Test the tool" lets the reader confirm
coverage at a glance, while "Installation", "How to configure",
"Testing your setup" forces three decoding styles for one list. Fix
order: first make each heading truthfully describe its section, then
regularize the forms.

## One Job per Paragraph

A paragraph makes one move: states one topic, develops it, and stops
when the next topic starts. The topic sentence goes first, states the
move plainly, and the remaining sentences either support it or get
cut.

### Rationale

Paragraphs are the reader's breathing units — a paragraph break
promises "you may pause here; nothing in the next block continues
this thought". When a second topic enters a paragraph, that promise
breaks: skimmers miss the second topic, and careful readers lose the
place. The fix is mechanical and cheap: whenever a sentence stops
being about the topic sentence's subject, start a new paragraph —
even a two-sentence paragraph is a paragraph. The mirror error is the
one-sentence paragraph habit, which flattens emphasis; a paragraph
break is an emphasis signal, and spending it on every sentence spends
it on nothing.
