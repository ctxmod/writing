# Close the Loop

The ending resolves the question the opening raised: it lands the
piece's claim in the reader's hands — an implication, an image, an
instruction — and stops. A tacked-on summary paragraph ("In
conclusion, we have seen that...") is the mark of a piece that never
decided what it was doing.

## Rationale

Openings and endings are one structure: the opening makes a promise
(specifically, the question it plants), and the ending is the
promise kept. A summary-ending re-narrates the middle instead of
closing the loop, and it reads as written-by-assembly — the piece's
parts listed rather than its effect completed. The strong close
returns to the opening's concrete material with what the middle
changed: the dead pipeline from the first paragraph appears again,
one sentence, carrying the assumption that got fixed. Endings that
add a new claim at the last moment ("and besides, the costs are
falling") open a loop at the exact point there is no room to close
one; if the claim matters, it belongs in the body. The final
sentence should be short, plain, and final — the reader should hear
the period.
