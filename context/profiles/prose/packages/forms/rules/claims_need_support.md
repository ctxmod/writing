# Claims Need Support

Every factual claim carries its evidence within reach — a figure, a
source, an example — or is visibly marked as judgment: "revenue
tripled in two years (company filings)" or "in my experience,
reviews stall on naming". The unsupported middle — stated as fact,
carried by nothing — is where a reader's trust quietly drains.

## Rationale

Readers do not demand citations for every line; they demand that
they can tell the difference between what the world will verify and
what the writer asserts. The two honest postures serve that: evidence
attached to fact, and visible authorship of opinion. What fails is
the disguise — opinion in the grammar of fact ("everyone knows
meetings are wasted", "it is widely accepted that remote work
fails") — because it borrows a consensus that does not exist, and a
reader who checks one such claim discounts the others. The support
scales with the claim: a broad claim ("most migrations fail")
carries more than a narrow one ("our March migration failed"), and a
claim doing argumentative work — the one a conclusion rests on —
gets the strongest support in the piece.
