# Openings Carry Weight

Open on the concrete: a scene, a fact, a question the reader can
feel — not a warm-up about the topic in general. "The night the
rendering pipeline died, we lost four hours and one assumption" is
an opening; "In today's fast-paced world of software, reliability is
important" is a held door.

## Rationale

The first two sentences decide whether the rest is read, and
throat-clearing introductions — "Since the dawn of...", "There are
many things to consider when...", "In this article, we will
explore..." — spend that decision on nothing. The concrete opening
works because it creates a specific question in the reader's mind
(how did it die? which assumption?) that the piece then earns the
right to answer; abstractions create no question because they
commit to nothing. The test: could the opening open a hundred other
pieces? If yes, it is not an opening, it is a category label. Write
the opening last if needed — after the piece knows what it is, the
door that leads in is easy to find.
